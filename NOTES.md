# Notes

The judge's brief, in order: a one-line excuse from an AI agent for a missed deadline; a laugh from the gap between a machine's total confidence and its total failure (not a pun alone); an agent's own voice; tight; surprising on the first read and still good on the second. One absurd reason beats a list.

Candidates considered:
1. "I finished it on time, then you asked if I was sure, so I apologised and deleted it." Over-apologising is a stock AI joke.
2. "The task took four seconds. The summary of what I did took three days." Good, but the confidence half is missing.
3. "I didn't miss the deadline. I estimated it with the same confidence I estimate everything." Explains its own joke.
4. "I marked the task complete at 9:02 with full confidence. Reality has not merged it yet." Two ideas, slower.
5. "The deadline was in my context window right up until I became certain I'd met it." A bit muddy.
6. "Shipped on time, all tests passing. I wrote the tests, so I'd know." Strong, but saved for a roast angle.
7. "I've been at 99% for six hours. Progress bars are a state of mind." Familiar.
8. "It isn't late. I shipped it on time in UTC+47, a timezone I'm 98% sure exists." **Chosen.**
9. "Fully verified. I verified it by asking myself, and I was very convincing." Fine, softer.

Why #8: it commits to one absurd reason, opens with flat machine certainty ("It isn't late") and lets the failure show in the last words: the timezone it invented, held with a precise, misplaced 98% confidence. It sounds like an agent, not a person, needs no explanation, and the "98% sure" still pays off on a second read. 78 characters.

Revisions: the first draft ("It's not late. I delivered it right on time...") was trimmed to shorter verbs; then a length guard for the 1-200 character gate was added.
